Measure whether ExactKV slows down or speeds up generation under a controlled diagnostic harness, and whether span verification reduces verifier overhead compared to sequential verification.

**Post–030b note:** Batched span verify is **active** on fp16 GPU (Exp 030b SDPA parity fix;
no blanket sequential fallback). Estimated span forwards are ~4× lower than sequential, but
mean wall-clock is **~10% slower** (span/sequential tok/s ratio **0.90**). Fewer verifier

## 16. What this proves

- Controlled diagnostic timing for four arms on a fixed 20-prompt panel (RunPod A5000, fp16).
- Exactness gate passed post–030b: sequential **0** / span **0** failures; span ≡ sequential outputs.
- Batched span verify exercised on fp16 GPU (no sequential fallback in this run).
- **ExactKV is ~2.67× slower than full greedy** in this diagnostic setup (~20.4 vs ~54.4 tok/s mean).
- Span is **~10% slower than sequential** on wall-clock despite ~75% fewer estimated verifier forwards.

## 17. What this does not prove

- Production serving throughput or latency.
- General speedup on other models, hardware, or prompt distributions.
- Active GPU memory savings.
- Model accuracy improvement.

## 18. Limitations

- Single model (Qwen2.5-0.5B) and small prompt panel (20 prompts).
- Diagnostic harness overhead (Python loop, deep-copy) included.
- Span speed blocker: batched verify reduces forward *count* but not wall-clock on this hardware/model.
- `lossy_only` arm is **not** exactness-preserving; timing for that arm is diagnostic only.
